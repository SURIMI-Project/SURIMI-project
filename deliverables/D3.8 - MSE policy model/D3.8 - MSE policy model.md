## 1. Model purpose
Management Strategy Evaluation (**MSE**) is a simulation-based approach used in fisheries management to test and compare alternative management strategies under uncertainty. In SURIMI it runs many simulations with varying assumptions about stock biomass, recruitment, catches, quotas, observation errors, and environmental conditions to evaluate which strategies are most likely to achieve objectives such as sustainable harvests and stock conservation.

The **MSE Policy model** is implemented by the **Fisheries Authority** service that is a component of the **SURIMI** simulation framework. SURIMI is a multi-model system that simulates marine fisheries ecosystems. All models — including this one — communicate exclusively with the **SURIMI Controller** over **gRPC**, using a shared Protobuf-based protocol (`BSR.Surimi.Surimi-Protocol`).

The Fisheries Authority model represents the regulatory authority in the simulation. It provides fishing regulations (Total Allowable Catches) to other models and receives updates on biomass, catch dispositions, and fishing activity from the Controller, which aggregates data from the ecology and fleet models.

## 3. Configuration
The Fisheries Authority service needs to be configured to be able to do its calculations. The configuration depends on the scenario (nortwestern_med, anchovy_bay or aegean_sea) which is passed at the beginning of the simulation.
At that moment, the configuration files are downloaded from the corresponding directory in the S3 bucket, into the Kubernetes pod. And from there they are used by the Fisheries Authority service.
The following configuration files are necessary:

### 3.1 Recruitment
The estimated growth of the biomass is calculated via simple stock-recruitment curve. These species-specific curves are configured via three parameters:
* RstockRatio: Determines the overall height (maximum recruitment level) of the curve.
* RHalfB0Ratio: Determines the position of the half-recruitment point (orange vertical line).
* cvRec: Does not change the curve itself; it controls year-to-year recruitment variability around the curve.

The MSE recruitment logic was lifted from the Ecopath with Ecosim (EwE) software, where the graph and table below are used to configure the recruitment dynamics. For a description of the underlying calculations see [Szurma et al. (2018)](https.doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0196307).

## 4. Inputs

### 4.1 InitialiseSimulation
[Buf specification](https://buf.build/surimi/surimi-protocol/docs/main%3Asurimi.v1#surimi.v1.FisheriesAuthorityService.InitialiseSimulation)

This message contains the contract of the simulation, with the list of Species and Fleets etc. that are part of the scenario. This is also the moment the configuration files are downloaded from the S3 bucket and validated. If the configuration file contains a Species or a fleet that is not part of the contract, the simulation is canceled and a descriptive error message is logged.


### 4.2 CreateRegulations
[Buf specification](https://buf.build/surimi/surimi-protocol/docs/main%3Asurimi.v1#surimi.v1.FisheriesAuthorityService.CreateRegulations)

At the start of the simulation, the CreateRegulations message is sent to the Fisheries Authority service. It contains the Harvest control rules with which the Total Allowable Catch is calculated at the beginning of the year.

With a harvest control rule, the Fisheries Authority can implement a feedback mechanism that adjusts TACs based on observed biomass levels. For example, if the biomass of a particular species falls below a predefined threshold, the harvest control rule may reduce the TAC for that species to prevent overfishing. Conversely, if biomass is above the threshold, the TAC may be increased to allow for sustainable harvesting.

The image below shows a harvest control rule as configured in Ecopath with Ecosim for the Atlantic Bluefin Tuna (BFT).

<img src="harvest_control_rule.png" alt="Harvest Control Rule" width="600"/>

Only for the species that have harvest control rules are taken into account in the quota calculation. For the other species, there is no quota.

In the table at the botom of the image you can see that each harvest control rule can be described by 3 numbers:

* Biomass limit (the lower limit of the biomass. When the biomass is below this, no fishing is allowed)
* Biomass base (the upper limit of the biomass. When the biomass is more then this, fishing is unrestricted)
* F max (the maximum allowed catch)

### 4.3 UpdateBiomass
[Buf specification](https://buf.build/surimi/surimi-protocol/docs/main%3Asurimi.v1#surimi.v1.FisheriesAuthorityService.UpdateBiomass)

Every month the Biomass of every applicable species is saved so it can be used in the calculation.  

### 4.4 Update CatchDisposition (total)
[Buf specification](https://buf.build/surimi/surimi-protocol/docs/main%3Asurimi.v1#surimi.v1.FisheriesAuthorityService.UpdateCatchDisposition)

The `"gross catch" - "live discards"` of all the cells is summed up, per species. This is aggregated every month, so when the new year starts the total amount of fish that has been harvested can be used in the calculation. 


## 5. Outputs


### 5.1 GetRegulations
[Buf specification](https://buf.build/surimi/surimi-protocol/docs/main%3Asurimi.v1#surimi.v1.FisheriesAuthorityService.GetRegulations)

At the start of every regulatory year, the new quota for each applicable species is calculated and transmitted to the Ecopath (EwE) service and the POSEIDON service. This process is carried out within the `GetRegulations` message, issued by the SURIMI Controller.

Upon receipt of this message, the Fisheries Authority first reviews the performance of each fish stock over the preceding twelve months, using the biomass data accumulated during that period. This information is submitted to the stock assessment procedure (`DoAssessment`), which evaluates the current state of each population. It is at this stage that stochastic (random) uncertainty is first introduced into the process, as the assessment incorporates a coefficient of variation to simulate observational error in the biomass estimate.

Where the simulation is in its first regulatory year, insufficient historical data exists to support a model-derived calculation. In this case, the Authority applies the pre-configured initial quotas defined in the scenario configuration. In all subsequent years, the Authority instead relies on the outcome of the assessment and delegates the quota calculation to the MSE (Management Strategy Evaluation) model via `UpdateQuotas`. It is within this step that a second source of random variation is applied, representing uncertainty in the quota-setting process itself, before the final Total Allowable Catch per species is determined.

Following the quota calculation, the accumulated catch totals for the year are cleared, allowing the next regulatory year to begin its accounting from zero.

Finally, the calculated quota for each species is apportioned across the applicable fishing fleets — each identified by gear type and country — in accordance with the quota shares defined in the scenario. The resulting set of Total Allowable Catches, specified per species and per fleet, is returned through the Controller, which subsequently distributes this information to:

- **Ecopath**, the ecology model responsible for simulating the response of fish populations and the broader ecosystem; and
- **POSEIDON**, the fleet model responsible for simulating how individual vessels and fleets adapt their fishing behaviour in response to the newly imposed limits.

In this manner, the quota determined for the coming year directly influences the subsequent behaviour of the simulated marine ecosystem.
