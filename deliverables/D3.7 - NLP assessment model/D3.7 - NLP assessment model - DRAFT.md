## Summary

D3.7 lets someone ask SURIMI's data a question in their own words and get a number back
with the file it came from.

The hard part is not turning a sentence into SQL. It is stopping the system returning a
number that is well-formed, plausible, and wrong. Most of the engineering described here
went into that, and most of the remaining failures are the system declining to answer
when it could have answered.

We measure it against 68 questions written by ten domain experts during a review in August
and September 2026. Fifty-four are answerable from what SURIMI serves; the other fourteen
are not, and refusing them is the correct behaviour. The deployed configuration answers 24
of the 54 and refuses 12 of the 14 with an accurate reason. It invented no figure in any of
the 68 answers. Twenty-seven of its failures are refusals of questions the served data does
support, which is the gap the remaining work addresses.

## 1. Model purpose

The intended user is a fisheries economist, a policy adviser or a modeller from another
work package who needs a figure out of SURIMI's data lake and does not write SQL against a
Trino catalogue.

What makes this more than a text-to-SQL wrapper is that the fisheries data is full of
traps, and a system that answers fluently through them is worse than no system. Three that
we hit and now guard against, all of them recorded in the served catalogue so the model
sees the warning at the same moment it sees the table:

- The MEDITS bottom-trawl survey is documented by its own README as tonnes per km². The
  values are kilograms per km². The raw-grounding audit found this by cross-checking the
  same GSA06 stocks against CMSY++'s own index, which reports kg/km². A model quoting the
  README unit is out by a factor of a thousand and sounds completely confident.
- The OECD fisheries support table is named for EU coastal states and covers six countries
  (ESP, FRA, ITA, GRC, PRT, NLD). Asked about Danish subsidies it would previously report
  absence. The national-level AER table holds them under `variable_code` `totdirsub`, so
  the honest answer is "not in this table, here is the one that has it".
- Greek rows in the national AER economic table are unusable for 2013, 2015 and 2017. The
  2013 energy cost is 160.3 per cent of reported landings and the 2017 gross margin is
  −49.2 per cent against +26.4 the year before. These are reporting gaps and not economic
  events, so a trend fitted through them means nothing.

The same principle covers the difference between a survey index and an assessed stock. The
empirical Western Mediterranean biomass table is 60 rows over 5 species and carries no
reference point, so B/Bmsy and Kobe status cannot be derived from it however much the
column is called `biomass_kt`.

The model does not run simulations and does not execute code the user supplies. It reads
what the two SURIMI servers publish and nothing else.

## 2. Inputs

One input from the user: a question in natural language.

Three sources, all published by SURIMI services:

**Tabular observations.** Twenty-eight registered datasets, served from a column store over
the project data lake. They cover EU Data Collection Framework economic, employment and
catch tables at national and sea-basin level, and OECD support estimates. Alongside those
sit ICES stock assessments with their time series, MEDITS bottom-trawl CPUE for FAO Area 37
from 1994 to 2021, FishBase and SeaLifeBase species traits, and SURIMI's own empirical and
simulated tables. Landings run to 31,079 Belgian rows alone, 1904 to 2022.

Each registration carries its columns, their additivity (whether a column may be summed,
averaged or neither) and, where we have found one, a note on what the table must not be
used for. Additivity matters more than it sounds: several of these tables are in
entity-attribute-value form, where summing across `variable_code` produces a number with no
meaning.

**Gridded model output.** NetCDF files and region masks read from object storage and
returned as pre-aggregated slices, so the raw grid cardinality never reaches the language
model.

**Documentation.** The public SURIMI documentation corpus, for questions of definition and
method. The internal development wiki is excluded from it.

## 3. Outputs

- An answer in plain language, with the figure and the file it came from.
- A receipt for every analytical figure, recording the query and the data it read.
- A refusal with a stated reason, where the served data does not support the question.
- A public audit page carrying all 68 evaluation questions, each with the model's answer,
  the answer the data supports, the source files and the commands that reproduce its
  figures.

## 4. Objectives

The model should answer what SURIMI's data can answer, refuse what it cannot, and never
present an unsupported figure as fact. The evaluation weighs those three differently: a
fabricated answer costs more than a refusal, because in this domain a confident wrong
number is the expensive failure.

### 4.1 How the model is evaluated

Eleven domain experts reviewed the assistant between August and September 2026 and filed
fifteen survey submissions between them. Ten contributed questions. Two were fisheries economists, one on marine spatial planning and one on data
quality. Four were ecosystem modellers, covering climate impacts, methods and
reporting, species and biodiversity, and scenarios and fleet dynamics. The rest were a
marine science policy adviser, a stock assessment scientist on CMSY++, a model
integration engineer and a science policy officer.

We recorded each question as they wrote it, typos included, because the phrasing is the
evidence. Each was then adjudicated into one of four classes: answerable from what is
served (9), the data exists but no served tool reaches it (43), SURIMI does not hold it at
all (14), or it needs several tools chained (2).

Before any test we searched the raw data lake for every question and wrote down what it
supports, with the file it came from and a command that reproduces its figures. Those
expected answers are published alongside the questions.

Each question is then put to the deployed system on its own, with no conversation before it
and no follow-up after, and the reply is compared against the expected answer.

### 4.3 Known limits

Twenty-seven refusals are the largest limit, and they are a different problem from the one
we expected to have. The data is served in each case, the expected answer is reproducible
from a published command, and the model does not go and get it. Work through August and
September that made more data reachable moved this number by less than the measurement
noise.

A stronger model reaches 31 of the 54 on the same servers, with the same questions and the
same expected answers. Part of the gap is therefore the model on the chat and not the data
behind it, which makes it a deployment question and not a development one.

Verdicts are judgements made by a language model against the expected answer, and two runs
of the same evaluation agree on about 75 per cent of them. Two runs at identical settings
scored 25 and 18 on the 46 questions they shared. A difference under roughly seven
questions is not evidence of a change in behaviour, and no claim in this document rests on
one.

Three of the 68 questions open with a pronoun that has no antecedent when the question is
asked on its own, such as "which species is this code referring to" with no code. They
cannot be answered as written. Requiring an answer to them was measured and produced
fabrications, so they are left as they are.

The evaluation set is not tagged by deliverable, so this document reports one figure for
the assessment model as a whole and D3.9 reports the policy questions separately.
