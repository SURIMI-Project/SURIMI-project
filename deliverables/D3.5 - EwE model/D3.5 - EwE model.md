## Summary

## 1. Model purpose
Marine ecosystem models help improve our understanding of the impacts of human activities, natural phenomena, and climate change on marine food webs (Heymans et al., 2020; Stow et al., 2009). Offering the most comprehensive platforms to unify ecological processes with statistical insights and data-driven approaches (Ellis et al., 2020), complex, mechanistic ecosystem models are increasingly applied in ecological research, management advice, policy exploration, and environmental impact analysis under climate change scenarios (Borja et al., 2020; Fulton et al., 2015; Kytinou et al., 2020; Link et al., 2011; Peck et al., 2018; Serpetti et al., 2017). Ecosystem modelling integrates a wide range of disciplines (Fulton, 2010), including physical oceanography, biochemistry, food-web dynamics, risk analysis, decision making, economics and the social sciences (Figure 1).

Here, we describe how the computational core of the Ecopath with Ecosim (EwE) food web modelling approach was integrated into the SURIMI modeling framework. Ecopath with Ecosim (EwE) is a free ecological/ecosystem modeling software suite. EwE has three main components: Ecopath – a static, mass-balanced snapshot of the system; Ecosim – a time dynamic simulation module for policy exploration; and Ecospace – a spatial and temporal dynamic module primarily designed for exploring impact and placement of protected areas. The Ecopath software package can be used to address ecological questions, evaluate ecosystem effects of fishing, explore management policy options, analyze the impact and placement of marine protected areas, predict movement and accumulation of contaminants and tracers (Ecotracer), model effect of environmental changes, and facilitate end-to-end model construction.

In SURIMI, the integerated EwE computational core represents ecological dynamics under influence of fishing, fisheries measures, and climate change. 

By loading different EwE parameter sets into the computational core, the EwE model can represent different ecosystems. In SURIMI, these capabilities will be used to represent the North-western Mediterranean ecosystem (as described in this deliverable) and a simpler EwE model - the MICE model of the Aegean Sea (as described in SURIMI deliverable 3.4)

### Conceptual integration ###
The original EwE computational core was integrated into SURIMI in its entirety and did not require any changes to its computational code or internal data model. Instead, additional software was developed to translate SURIMI inputs into the terminology and level of aggregation understood by EwE, and to translate EwE model outputs back into the terminology and granularity required by SURIMI.

The main conceptual differences that needed to be reconciled were:

- **Biological entities:** SURIMI operates at the species level, whereas EwE operates on functional groups. An EwE functional group may represent an aggregation of multiple species, a single species, or a particular life stage of a species.
- **Fishing activity:** SURIMI represents fisheries through fishing-gear segments, whereas EwE provides considerably more flexibility in how fleets and fishing effort can be represented.
- **Temporal resolution:** Both SURIMI and EwE operate on monthly time steps, so no temporal aggregation or disaggregation is required.

To automate the exchange of information between SURIMI and EwE, the EwE modeller provides a semantic mapping between the entities defined in the SURIMI scenario contract and the corresponding entities within the EwE model. This mapping is model-specific and defines, for example, how SURIMI species and fishing segments relate to EwE functional groups and fleets.

Using this mapping, the **EwE controller**—the software component that encapsulates EwE within the SURIMI workflow—performs the required translations at runtime. Its responsibilities include:

- incorporating POSEIDON fisheries dynamics into those EwE fleets that are controlled by POSEIDON (or hypthetically any other dedicated fisheries model);
- maintaining an internal estimate of species composition within each EwE functional group and spatial cell;
- translating species-level catches generated by POSEIDON into removals from the corresponding EwE functional-group biomass;
- translating catches calculated by EwE at the functional-group level back into species-level catches, discards, and yields for reporting to the SURIMI framework; and
- translating fish prices, given per species and per market segment, to the prices per functional group.

Because EwE does not explicitly represent individual species within an aggregated functional group, the controller maintains an approximate species composition for each spatial cell. This composition is initially based on the taxonomic proportions assigned to the corresponding EwE functional group.

When POSEIDON removes a particular species from a cell, the catch is deducted from the biomass of the corresponding EwE functional group and the local species composition is adjusted accordingly. Over subsequent monthly time steps, the composition is allowed to recover towards its default Ecopath proportions according to the growth rates assigned to the individual species.

This provides SURIMI with an approximate representation of species-level change while leaving the underlying EwE functional-group dynamics unchanged.

## 3. Inputs

### InitialiseSimulation message (init)
The configuration of the EwE run is passed in the InitialiseSimulation message. It also includes the scenario contract.
Internally, the EwE controller will:
- Initialize the EwE modelling engine;
- Load the available EwE model (Ecopath, Ecosim scenario, Ecospace scenario) as requested in the contract;
- Set the Ecospace model up with external time series of environmental data as requested in the contract;
- Check semnatic mappings, provided with the EwE model, against the scenario contract and set up EwE for SURIMI information exchange;
- Run the Ecospace model spin-up period, and continue running until the first SURIMI simulation year is reached.
Then the EwE controller pauses for futher instructions.

### UpdateRegulations message
The harvest control rules are sent to EwE.
The EwE controller reads the harvest control rules

### UpdatePrices message
The market prices are sent to EwE
These prices are applied to EwE Ecopath base prices, per functional group.

### UpdateCatchDisposition message
The catches of POSEIDON, per-species, are sent to EwE. 
These catches are deducted from the biomass pool of effected functional groups in affected cells.
